A Core Argument · Difference-Analysis Positioning 3 questions

Why difference-analysis instead of the fidelity approach? Wouldn't a more human-like AI Persona be more useful?

The fidelity approach presupposes that humans are the standard AI should imitate. In our data, AI Persona is +43% on structural dimensions but -79% on authenticity dimensions — this isn't a defect, it's a structural difference. Treating the gap as noise erases the signal.

By analyzing the difference directly, we can position AI Persona as a contrastive baseline that makes the hesitation, contradiction, and emotional flow in human interviews recognizable as cognitive signals rather than noise.

openerThe fidelity approach quietly assumes the human is the target. We flip that — the difference itself is what's informative.
You call this "synthetic thinking" vs "experiential thinking." Isn't this just repackaging what we already know — that LLMs hallucinate?

Hallucination is about output accuracy; synthetic thinking is about cognitive process. They're at different levels. Hallucination says the answer is wrong. Synthetic thinking describes how the answer is assembled instantly from training patterns, without the temporal, fragmented, emotion-permeated character of human cognition.

This distinction matters because it reframes AI's over-completeness not as a flaw to fix, but as a different mode of cognition — which changes how we should use it in research.

Why do we need to interview an AI Persona at all — if we can just ask it for the answer directly?

Two reasons. First, interviewing it produces designerly artifacts — narrative fragments, tone, hesitation patterns — that a direct Q&A wouldn't surface. Second, it makes the AI Persona legible to a design team, not just to the researcher. The team can meet the character in dialogue and form intuitions together.

Direct Q&A gives you an answer; interviewing gives you a character with which the team can build shared understanding. That's still valuable, even if the character is AI.

riskThis is the sharpest question. Rehearse it. If they push, admit that direct-Q&A shortcuts many use cases, and reposition your contribution as "when the artifact of interviewing matters."

B Method Rigor · Sample, Coder, Fairness 3 questions

Only 2 human participants — how can you make a comparison claim from that?

This is a qualitative structural analysis, not a statistical comparison. Our unit of analysis isn't the person but the segment — three design concepts × four questions per person gave us 12 segments per participant. Combined with the six-dimension coding, that's 216 data points identifying structural patterns.

The claim isn't "AI differs from all humans by 43%" — it's "in this comparable context, AI shows a consistently different cognitive profile that is worth investigating further."

A single coder for the linguistic analysis — how do you defend the reliability?

We acknowledge this is a limitation. Our mitigation: each of the six dimensions has concrete behavioral indicators — for example, C2 counts filler words per 100 words, C6 measures the time between question and reflection. Edge cases were re-anchored against prior examples to prevent drift.

For future work, a second independent coder is the natural next step. But the effect sizes here (43% and -79%) are large enough that a second coder would likely reproduce the direction, if not the exact magnitude.

gambitAcknowledge honestly, then reframe: large effects don't disappear under a second coder.
Your humans spoke over voice; your AI Persona typed. That's an unfair comparison — the medium alone would explain the differences.

You're right that the medium is a confound, and we should surface that more clearly. But two observations: first, several of our authenticity markers — emoji use in place of emotion words, zero latency to reflection, absence of self-repair — reflect AI's response construction, not just the input modality.

Second, this is exactly the kind of setup a designer would encounter today — a text-only AI vs a spoken interview — so the comparison reflects ecological validity. Still, testing an AI Persona in voice mode is important future work.

riskDon't deny it — concede the confound and reframe as ecological validity.

C Related Work Positioning 2 questions

How does your six-dimension script differ from Park et al.'s generative agents in 2023?

Three layers of difference. Purpose — Park's work simulated 25 agents interacting in a sandbox to produce emergent social behavior; ours builds one persona for a designer to interview in depth. Framework layer — Park provides an agent runtime architecture (memory-reflection-planning); ours provides a persona construction methodology for the researcher. Park's seed persona is a paragraph; ours is a systematic six-dimension framework.

Linguistic style — for Park, language style is an emergent property of the LLM; for us, it's a controlled dimension we intentionally source from community corpora. That's the deliberate researcher-side move.

Recent work like PersonaCraft (Jung 2025) and Silicon Sampling (Argyle 2023) already generate personas at scale. What's new here?

Those works focus on generating personas at scale or using LLMs to simulate survey responses — evaluated on fidelity, i.e., how closely they mirror real human data. Our contribution is orthogonal: instead of asking "how close to human?", we ask "where do they structurally diverge, and what does that gap mean for design research practice?"

The novelty is in the reframing: AI Persona as a contrastive baseline rather than a substitute — which is actionable for design teams in a way "n = 1000 synthetic users" isn't.

D Synthetic vs Experiential Thinking 2 questions

You claim "no better or worse" — but structural clarity is clearly a strength. Isn't your framing avoiding the hard question?

Structural clarity is a strength for certain design tasks — early exploration, hypothesis generation, concept testing. Authenticity is a strength for others — late-stage validation, uncovering unarticulated needs, building empathy. Neither dominates across all tasks.

The "no better or worse" framing is not neutrality — it's task-dependent value. We're saying: match the mode to the task, don't rank the modes.

Once LLMs improve, won't AI develop something like "experiential thinking" too? Your framework may age quickly.

The framework describes current architectural realities, not permanent limits. If future models genuinely develop temporal, embodied, emotion-permeated cognition, we'd need to re-evaluate.

But note: many recent "more human-like" LLM efforts still simulate hesitation rather than experience it. Adding artificial delays or filler words doesn't change the underlying synchronous-assembly cognition. The distinction may be more architectural than trainable.

gambitConcede on principle, then reassert: simulated hesitation ≠ experienced cognition.

E Application to Design Practice 2 questions

Has any actual design team adopted your "AI breadth + human depth" model? Or is this still speculative?

The complementary model is derived from our findings, and I don't yet have field-deployment evidence at scale. But the pattern is already emerging in practice — teams use AI to draft initial user hypotheses, then validate with a smaller number of human interviews.

Our contribution is providing a principled reason for this split, based on the two modes of thinking — rather than treating it as a resource compromise.

If designers start with AI Personas, aren't they baking in bias that shapes what they hear from real users later?

Yes — that's a real risk, and it's why the critical stance matters. In our PPS results, the credibility score was the lowest (3.67 / 4.17 out of 7). That's a healthy signal — testers were able to identify what AI was getting wrong.

We recommend explicitly framing AI Persona output as hypotheses to be tested, not conclusions — which is how the "AI first, then human" sequence is meant to work. The AI's role is to widen the search space, not to define the answer.

F Limitations & Generalizability 2 questions

Your study is on Taiwanese Gen Z ETF investors. How does this generalize beyond that context?

The specific findings — that Anne says "haha" as punctuation, that community language matters — are context-specific. But the structural patterns — AI's zero-latency reflection, absence of self-repair, over-complete narratives — are architectural features of LLMs, likely to generalize across languages and domains.

Applying the six-dimension methodology to other contexts (healthcare, legal, grief work) is explicit future work — and would test whether the patterns hold.

LLMs move so fast — will your findings still be relevant in six months?

The specific effect sizes may shift as models improve. But our findings are a snapshot under specific technical conditions, and we frame them that way.

What we expect to persist is the methodological approach — using structural difference as a design-research lens — and the conceptual framework of synthetic vs experiential thinking. Even if AI closes the authenticity gap, the framework tells us what to look for to detect that shift.

G Curveballs · Unexpected Angles 1 question

If Anne is a "researcher-constructed artifact," how is she different from a stereotype? What makes her a persona and not a caricature?

The difference is where the material comes from. A stereotype pulls from cultural assumptions and researcher intuition. Anne is grounded in secondary research (Gen Z ETF investor profiles) plus community corpora (30-50 first-person posts from PTT and Dcard). Her language, decision patterns, and emotional beats are traceable to source material.

She's still a researcher-mediated construction — I don't hide that in the paper. But the mediation is disciplined by real texts rather than by imagination alone. That's the same discipline that separates a well-built traditional persona from a caricature.
